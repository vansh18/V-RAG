NAKAMURA V. CALLOWAY STATE COLLEGE
Nakamura, Plaintiff,
v.
Calloway State College, Defendant.

Citation: 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991)
Court: United States District Court for the District of Fentress
Decision Date: 1991-02-11
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Owen Pryce
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Voss v. Milford University, 539 U.S. 344 (2003)
Topic: Point-Bonus Systems

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Kenji Nakamura sued Calloway State College in the United States District Court
for the District of Fentress, alleging that the College's point-bonus admissions index
violated Calloway v. Ashworth State University's narrow-tailoring requirement because it
assigned race a fixed numerical value rather than treating it as an unweighted factor
within a holistic review.

Facts
-----

Calloway State College uses a 150-point admissions index. Applicants receive points for
class rank, standardized test scores, extracurricular leadership, geographic origin,
and, relevant here, twenty points for membership in a designated minority group -- a
larger bonus than the College awards for any other single factor, including a perfect
standardized test score, which yields twelve points. Nakamura, who narrowly missed the
index cutoff for admission, argued the twenty-point racial bonus assigned race a fixed
numerical weight inconsistent with Calloway.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an admissions index that assigns race a fixed point value, while also assigning
point values to other factors, satisfies Calloway's requirement that race be considered
without a fixed numerical weight.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The court reasons that Calloway's concern was with race operating as a hidden or
unbounded thumb on the scale, not with numerical scoring as such. Because Calloway
State's index assigns a fixed point value to every factor considered -- not merely race
-- the court finds the twenty-point racial bonus no more constitutionally suspect than
the twelve points awarded for a top standardized test score or the points awarded for
extracurricular leadership.

The court acknowledges that the twenty-point bonus is larger than the points awarded for
any other single factor, but finds this difference in magnitude does not, standing
alone, transform an otherwise individualized index into a quota, so long as the index
continues to function as one component of an admissions decision that also considers
each applicant's full file.

The court finds the index's transparency to be, if anything, a point in its favor:
because the weight assigned to every factor, including race, is disclosed and fixed in
advance, the index avoids the risk of arbitrary or inconsistent application that a
wholly discretionary process might present.

The court declines to adopt a categorical rule against point-based admissions indices,
reasoning that Calloway's narrow-tailoring test asks whether race is genuinely one
factor among many, not whether the factors are expressed in narrative or numerical form.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion follows that authority. Reads calloway's narrow-tailoring test to permit numerical scoring so long as race is not uniquely or exclusively weighted.

Holding
-------

The district court holds that because every factor in the College's index -- not only
race -- receives a fixed point value, the index is a permissible, transparent method of
individualized review rather than a disguised quota, and satisfies narrow tailoring.

Conclusion
----------

The court enters judgment for Calloway State College.
