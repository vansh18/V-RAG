ODOM V. HARWICK COLLEGE
Odom, Plaintiff,
v.
Harwick College, Defendant.

Citation: 745 F. Supp. 3d 233 (D. Fentress 2026)
Court: United States District Court for the District of Fentress
Decision Date: 2026-05-29
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Priyanka Deshmukh
Status: active
Topic: Race-Neutral Alternatives

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Marcus Odom -- no relation to the plaintiff in Odom v. Milford University --
sued Harwick College, alleging that its post-Harmon admissions preference for applicants
from underrepresented geographic regions and first-generation college students was, like
the legacy preferences challenged in Ibarra, an unlawful proxy for race.

Facts
-----

After Harmon, Harwick College -- the same institution whose insufficiently
individualized process was remanded in Whitcombe v. Harwick College decades earlier --
adopted a preference for applicants from designated underrepresented geographic regions
within the state and for first-generation college students, criteria that do not
reference race and are administered without regard to any applicant's race, though the
record shows the designated regions have a somewhat higher proportion of minority
residents than the state as a whole.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a race-neutral geographic and first-generation-status preference, with some
correlation to regional demographics, violates the Equal Protection Clause after Harmon.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The court applies the framework set out in Park v. Calloway State College, finding that
Harwick's preference, like the socioeconomic preference upheld there, does not reference
race in its criteria and is not administered with regard to any applicant's race.

The court finds the correlation between the designated regions and regional minority
population somewhat higher than the correlation at issue in Park, but concludes that
this difference in degree does not change the analysis, because the College's stated
purpose -- expanding access for students from underserved regions and for first-
generation college students -- is independently substantiated and does not depend on any
racial classification.

The court distinguishes this case from Ibarra v. Ashworth University's discussion of
deliberate proxy use, finding no evidence in this record that Harwick selected the
designated regions because of their racial composition rather than because of the
independent, race-neutral factors -- rural isolation, limited local college-preparatory
resources -- the College's admissions office documented in adopting the preference.

The court notes, as Park did, that this holding would not necessarily extend to a case
presenting clearer evidence that a facially neutral criterion was selected specifically
because of, rather than merely correlated with, race.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Park v. Calloway State College, 712 F. Supp. 3d 90 (D. Fentress 2025) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies park's framework for facially race-neutral preferences.
* Ibarra v. Ashworth University, 730 F. Supp. 3d 501 (D. Fentress 2025) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes the deliberate-proxy concern discussed in ibarra.
* Whitcombe v. Harwick College, 481 F.3d 950 (8th Cir. 2007) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Notes the college's much earlier, unrelated admissions litigation.

Holding
-------

The district court holds that Harwick's geographic and first-generation-status
preference is lawful, following Park v. Calloway State College's reasoning that a
facially race-neutral criterion does not become a racial classification merely because
it has some statistical correlation with race.

Conclusion
----------

The court enters judgment for Harwick College.
