REYES V. TRUSTEES OF ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Reyes, Petitioner,
v.
Trustees of Ashworth University, Respondent.

Citation: 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2003-06-23
Opinion Type: Dissent
Author: Justice Grant Halvorsen
Status: active
Topic: Strict Scrutiny - Colorblind Constitution

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Marisol Reyes, denied admission to Ashworth University, sued in federal
district court, which upheld the University's admissions program under Calloway v.
Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) (fictional). The Fourth Circuit affirmed.
This Court granted certiorari to resolve the disagreement among the courts of appeals
reflected in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989)
(fictional), on one hand, and Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771
(5th Cir. 1994) (fictional), and Nakamura v. Westbrook College of Law, 121 F.3d 355 (6th
Cir. 1997) (fictional), on the other.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University's admissions office reviews each application individually,
considering race as one factor among many with the stated goal of enrolling a 'critical
mass' of students from underrepresented groups. University officials testified that
critical mass is assessed qualitatively, by reference to whether students from
underrepresented groups report feeling able to participate in campus discourse without
serving as a token representative of their group, rather than by reference to any fixed
percentage or number.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the 'critical mass' concept is meaningfully different from a quota, and whether
it can be administered by courts in a principled way.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Dissenting Opinion
------------------

Justice Halvorsen argues that the majority's 'critical mass' concept cannot be
meaningfully distinguished from the fixed set-asides this Court struck down in Delgado.
Although Ashworth University disclaims any fixed numerical target, Justice Halvorsen
notes that admissions officials must, as a practical matter, form some working sense of
what number or percentage of underrepresented students would constitute critical mass,
whether or not that number is ever written down.

He criticizes the majority's qualitative framing -- avoiding 'tokenism,' enabling
students to participate 'without serving as a token representative' -- as inherently
resistant to judicial review, because no reviewing court can meaningfully test whether a
university's undisclosed, internal sense of adequate representation has, in fact,
crossed into an unlawful quota.

Justice Halvorsen would require any institution invoking the diversity rationale to
disclose, and to defend with data, the actual target it is pursuing, reasoning that
transparency is the only way strict scrutiny's narrow-tailoring requirement can be
meaningfully enforced rather than merely recited.

He also joins the reasoning of Justice Vance's earlier dissents in Whitfield and
Calloway insofar as they express skepticism that any judicially administrable line
exists between the individualized review the majority approves and the quotas this Court
has repeatedly condemned.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Delgado v. Trent Institute of Technology, 538 F.2d 902 (3d Cir. 1976) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Argues critical mass cannot be meaningfully distinguished from the set-aside struck down in delgado.
* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Joins the skepticism expressed in justice vance's calloway dissent.

Holding
-------

Justice Halvorsen dissents, arguing that 'critical mass' is an unworkable and
unreviewable quota by another name.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Halvorsen respectfully dissents.
