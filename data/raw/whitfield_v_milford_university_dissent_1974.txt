WHITFIELD V. BOARD OF REGENTS OF MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Whitfield, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Regents of Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 417 U.S. 226 (1974)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 1974-06-10
Opinion Type: Dissent
Author: Justice Hollis Vance
Status: active
Topic: Strict Scrutiny - Colorblind Constitution

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Eleanor Whitfield, a rejected applicant to Milford University's undergraduate
program, filed suit in the United States District Court for the District of Fentress
alleging that the University's consideration of race in admissions violated the Equal
Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. The district court applied the rational-
basis standard set out in Marsh v. Board of Regents of Calloway State College, 412 F.2d
118 (4th Cir. 1971) (fictional), and entered judgment for the University. The Fourth
Circuit affirmed on the same rationale. This Court granted certiorari to resolve whether
race-conscious admissions programs in public higher education are subject to strict
scrutiny.

Facts
-----

Milford University's admissions office reviews each application individually and
considers an applicant's race as one factor among several in assembling an entering
class the University describes as diverse along multiple dimensions, including but not
limited to race. No fixed number of seats is set aside for any racial group, and race is
not assigned a predetermined numerical weight. Whitfield, who is white, was denied
admission in a year in which the University admitted a number of minority applicants
whose standardized test scores and grade point averages were, on the summary record
before the Court, lower than Whitfield's.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the Equal Protection Clause permits government actors to classify individuals by
race for any purpose, including the pursuit of educational diversity.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Dissenting Opinion
------------------

Justice Vance agrees with the majority that strict scrutiny applies but parts ways over
whether any interest -- diversity, remediation, or otherwise -- can be compelling enough
to justify classifying individual human beings by race. In his view, the Equal
Protection Clause protects persons, not groups, and its guarantee is dishonored the
moment the government sorts an individual applicant into a racial category and treats
that category as relevant to how the applicant is judged.

Justice Vance criticizes the majority's diversity rationale as resting on stereotype:
the unstated premise that members of a given racial group will predictably contribute a
particular viewpoint to campus discourse. That premise, he writes, is itself a form of
the very racial essentialism the Equal Protection Clause was designed to root out.

He is equally unpersuaded by the concurrence's remedial rationale, which he warns will
prove infinitely elastic in application: virtually any institution with a documented
history reaching back far enough will be able to locate some past exclusion to justify
present-day racial sorting, with no reliable judicial mechanism for determining when the
remedy has run its course.

Justice Vance would hold that Milford University's consideration of race, however
individualized and however well-intentioned, fails strict scrutiny because no compelling
interest can justify classifying an individual applicant by race, and he would reverse
outright rather than remand.

Holding
-------

Justice Vance dissents, arguing that the Constitution is colorblind and that no
interest, however sincerely held, can justify sorting individual applicants by race.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Vance respectfully dissents and would reverse the judgment below.
