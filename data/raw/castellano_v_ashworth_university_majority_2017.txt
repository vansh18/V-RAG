CASTELLANO V. ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Castellano, Plaintiff,
v.
Ashworth University, Defendant.

Citation: 872 F.3d 1188 (10th Cir. 2017)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit
Decision Date: 2017-06-02
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Marcus Castellane
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Workability of Race-Neutral Alternatives

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

This case arose in the Tenth Circuit, involving a different plaintiff's challenge to
Ashworth University's admissions process following remand proceedings related to
Ferreira v. Ashworth University, 779 F.3d 240 (2d Cir. 2015) (fictional), which prompted
Ashworth to develop an evidentiary record on race-neutral alternatives that was
introduced in this separate action.

Facts
-----

Following Ferreira, Ashworth University piloted three race-neutral alternatives over
four admissions cycles: a socioeconomic-status-based preference, a percentage plan for
top-ranked students from partner high schools, and an expanded outreach and recruitment
program. Ashworth's institutional research office documented that each alternative,
tested individually and in combination, produced a less diverse entering class than the
University's existing holistic process, without achieving comparable gains along other
admissions priorities.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a multi-year, evidence-based record of piloted race-neutral alternatives
satisfies the workability showing required by Ferreira.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel finds Ashworth's post-Ferreira record -- three alternatives, each piloted over
multiple admissions cycles, with documented results -- to be precisely the kind of
evidence-based showing Ferreira contemplated, in contrast to the untested, good-faith
assertions found insufficient there.

The panel credits the University's institutional research office's quantitative findings
that each piloted alternative produced measurably less diverse outcomes without
offsetting gains, and finds this evidence sufficient to establish that race-neutral
alternatives, at least as piloted, cannot achieve Ashworth's diversity objectives.

The panel expressly adopts Ferreira's workability framework as the correct elaboration
of Reyes and Calloway, and declines the plaintiff's invitation to require Ashworth to
continue piloting additional, hypothetical alternatives indefinitely, reasoning that
Ferreira's requirement of a genuine, evidence-based showing does not require an
institution to exhaust every conceivable alternative before it may exercise its
judgment.

The panel also finds Ashworth's continued file-specific documentation practices,
consistent with those credited in Delacroix and Park, and its periodic institutional
review, consistent with Calloway, sufficient to satisfy the remaining prongs of the
narrow-tailoring test.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Ferreira v. Ashworth University, 779 F.3d 240 (2d Cir. 2015) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies the workability requirement articulated in ferreira.
* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies reyes's underlying diversity rationale.
* Delacroix v. Brennan City University, 398 F.3d 611 (6th Cir. 2005) -- this opinion follows that authority. Follows delacroix's documentation approach.
* Park v. Trent Institute of Technology, 662 F. Supp. 2d 488 (D. Fentress 2009) -- this opinion follows that authority. Follows park's approach to periodic institutional review.

Holding
-------

The Tenth Circuit holds that Ashworth's detailed, multi-year record of piloted
alternatives satisfies Ferreira's workability requirement and upholds the University's
continued use of race-conscious review.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is AFFIRMED.
