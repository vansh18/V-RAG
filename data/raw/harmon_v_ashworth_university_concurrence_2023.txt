HARMON V. BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Harmon, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, Respondent.

Citation: 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2023-06-29
Opinion Type: Concurrence
Author: Justice Marcus Castellane
Status: active
Topic: Military Academy Carve-Out

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Diane Harmon, denied admission to Ashworth University, sued in federal
district court, urging the court to disregard Reyes and Calloway as wrongly decided. The
district court, bound by this Court's precedent, upheld Ashworth's admissions process,
and the Tenth Circuit affirmed on the strength of its own prior decision in Castellano
v. Ashworth University. This Court granted certiorari, together with Harmon v. Board of
Trustees of Milford University, to reconsider Whitfield, Calloway, Reyes, and Voss.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University's admissions process in 2023 is substantially the same qualitative,
individualized process this Court approved in Reyes in 2003 and the Tenth Circuit
approved, on a developed evidentiary record concerning race-neutral alternatives, in
Castellano in 2017. The parties do not dispute that Ashworth's process, if Reyes remains
good law, satisfies the narrow-tailoring framework Reyes, Ferreira, and Castellano
together describe. Harmon instead argues that Reyes and the diversity rationale on which
it rests should be overruled.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the majority's holding extends to the federal service academies, which have
historically invoked distinct national-security interests in support of race-conscious
admissions.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Concurring Opinion
------------------

Justice Castellane joins the Court's opinion in full but writes separately to flag an
issue the majority's reasoning does not squarely address: whether the distinct interests
asserted by the federal service academies, including interests related to unit cohesion
and national security historically invoked in that specific context, might be analyzed
differently than the general educational-diversity interest the Court rejects today.

He notes that no service academy is a party to this litigation and that the record
contains no evidence bearing on the academies' distinct interests, and emphasizes that
the Court's opinion should not be read either to resolve or to foreclose that separate
question.

Justice Castellane writes to ensure that lower courts, applying today's decision,
understand that its reasoning is addressed to civilian higher education and does not
purport to answer a question the Court has not been asked to decide.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Joins the majority opinion in full.

Holding
-------

Justice Castellane concurs in full, writing separately to note that the majority opinion
does not address, and does not resolve, whether a distinct national-security rationale
might apply differently to the federal service academies.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Castellane concurs.
