HARMON V. BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Harmon, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, Respondent.

Citation: 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2023-06-29
Opinion Type: Dissent
Author: Justice Thaddeus Okonkwo-Lindqvist
Status: active
Topic: Overruling of Diversity Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Diane Harmon, denied admission to Ashworth University, sued in federal
district court, urging the court to disregard Reyes and Calloway as wrongly decided. The
district court, bound by this Court's precedent, upheld Ashworth's admissions process,
and the Tenth Circuit affirmed on the strength of its own prior decision in Castellano
v. Ashworth University. This Court granted certiorari, together with Harmon v. Board of
Trustees of Milford University, to reconsider Whitfield, Calloway, Reyes, and Voss.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University's admissions process in 2023 is substantially the same qualitative,
individualized process this Court approved in Reyes in 2003 and the Tenth Circuit
approved, on a developed evidentiary record concerning race-neutral alternatives, in
Castellano in 2017. The parties do not dispute that Ashworth's process, if Reyes remains
good law, satisfies the narrow-tailoring framework Reyes, Ferreira, and Castellano
together describe. Harmon instead argues that Reyes and the diversity rationale on which
it rests should be overruled.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the majority's stare decisis analysis adequately justifies overruling fifty
years of precedent reaffirmed as recently as 2017 in Castellano.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Dissenting Opinion
------------------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist, who authored a concurrence in Reyes twenty years earlier
expressing hope that race-conscious review would eventually become unnecessary, writes
that the majority mistakes the ordinary process of doctrinal elaboration -- the kind of
refinement reflected in Ferreira's workability requirement and Castellano's evidentiary
framework -- for the kind of unworkability that justifies overruling precedent. In his
view, every complex area of constitutional doctrine requires elaboration over time, and
the existence of that elaboration does not show the underlying rule was mistaken.

He argues that the reliance interests the majority acknowledges but discounts are
substantial: institutions across the country built admissions systems, hired staff, and
developed the very documentation and workability practices credited in Delacroix, Park,
and Castellano in direct reliance on this Court's repeated reaffirmations of Whitfield's
diversity rationale, most recently in Castellano just six years before today's decision.

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist disputes the majority's characterization of Reyes's critical-
mass concept as unadministrable, noting that lower courts including the Sixth, Eighth,
and Tenth Circuits developed and applied increasingly precise standards -- file-specific
documentation in Delacroix, evidentiary workability showings in Ferreira and Castellano
-- that he views as evidence the doctrine was working, not failing.

He closes by expressing concern that the majority's suggestion that institutions may
consider how race has affected an applicant's life, while forbidding consideration of
race itself, draws a distinction he believes lower courts, including in the very cases
the majority cites, will find difficult to administer in practice.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Builds on his own earlier concurrence in reyes.
* Ferreira v. Ashworth University, 779 F.3d 240 (2d Cir. 2015) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Cites ferreira's workability requirement as evidence of successful doctrinal elaboration, not unworkability.
* Castellano v. Ashworth University, 872 F.3d 1188 (10th Cir. 2017) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Notes this court's own reaffirmance of the diversity rationale as recently as castellano.
* Delacroix v. Brennan City University, 398 F.3d 611 (6th Cir. 2005) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Cites delacroix's documentation framework as evidence the doctrine was administrable.

Holding
-------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist dissents, arguing that the majority overstates the diversity
rationale's unworkability, understates the reliance interests at stake, and abandons
precedent this Court reaffirmed as recently as 2017.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist respectfully dissents.
