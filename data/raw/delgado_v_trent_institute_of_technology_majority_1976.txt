DELGADO V. TRENT INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY
Delgado, Plaintiff,
v.
Trent Institute of Technology, Defendant.

Citation: 538 F.2d 902 (3d Cir. 1976)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit
Decision Date: 1976-03-02
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Marcus Feld
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Topic: Narrow Tailoring - Fixed Quotas

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Rosa Delgado, an applicant denied admission to Trent Institute of Technology's
engineering program, sued in the United States District Court for the Eastern District
of Pennshire, alleging that the Institute's fifteen-seat set-aside violated the Equal
Protection Clause as interpreted in Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University,
417 U.S. 226 (1974) (fictional). The district court upheld the program, reasoning that
Whitfield authorized any race-conscious admissions measure supported by a diversity
rationale. Delgado appealed to the Third Circuit.

Facts
-----

Trent Institute of Technology's engineering program reserved fifteen of one hundred
first-year seats for applicants from designated minority groups. Applications from those
seats were reviewed on a separate track, with a distinct and substantially lower cutoff
for standardized admissions-examination scores. Delgado, whose examination score
exceeded the cutoff applied to the general applicant pool but who was nonetheless
waitlisted while several reserved seats went to applicants with lower scores, challenged
the program as a fixed quota rather than an individualized, race-conscious factor of the
kind at issue in Whitfield.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University permits a fixed numerical
set-aside of admissions seats by race.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel reads Whitfield to permit consideration of race only as one factor within a
single, unified, individualized review process -- not as the basis for a categorically
separate admissions track with its own numerical target and its own, more lenient,
qualifying threshold. Trent Institute's program, in the panel's view, does not treat
race as one factor among many for any individual applicant; it treats race as the
threshold criterion determining which of two entirely different review processes an
applicant's file will receive.

The panel finds the fixed number of reserved seats -- fifteen, unchanged across the four
admissions cycles in the record -- particularly difficult to reconcile with Whitfield's
narrow-tailoring requirement, which the panel reads to demand a flexible, case-by-case
process rather than a predetermined numerical target that operates, in practical effect,
as a quota.

The panel is not persuaded by the Institute's argument that Whitfield's silence on set-
aside programs leaves the question open; in the panel's view, Whitfield's repeated
emphasis on individualized review of the whole applicant, rather than group-based
sorting into predetermined numerical categories, forecloses the Institute's program even
though Whitfield did not address a set-aside on its precise facts.

The panel remands with instructions that the Institute may continue to consider race as
one factor among many within a unified review process, consistent with Whitfield, but
may not operate a separate track with a fixed numerical target.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes whitfield's individualized 'one factor among many' approach from the institute's fixed, separately administered set-aside.

Holding
-------

The Third Circuit holds that Whitfield's endorsement of race as 'one factor among many'
does not extend to a fixed numerical set-aside administered on a wholly separate review
track, and that the Institute's program is not narrowly tailored.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is REVERSED and the case is REMANDED with
instructions to enjoin the fifteen-seat set-aside.
