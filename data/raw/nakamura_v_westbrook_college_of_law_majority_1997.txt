NAKAMURA V. WESTBROOK COLLEGE OF LAW
Nakamura, Plaintiff,
v.
Westbrook College of Law, Defendant.

Citation: 121 F.3d 355 (6th Cir. 1997)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit
Decision Date: 1997-05-20
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Selena Marchetti
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Topic: Circuit Split on Diversity Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Aiko Nakamura -- no relation to the plaintiff in Nakamura v. Calloway State
College -- sued Westbrook College of Law's Ohio branch campus in the United States
District Court for the Northern District of Fentress, challenging the branch campus's
admissions process. The district court upheld the process under Calloway and Ibarra. The
Sixth Circuit reviewed the appeal.

Facts
-----

Westbrook College of Law's branch campus uses a holistic admissions process resembling
the flagship campus's process upheld in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233
(9th Cir. 1989) (fictional). The Sixth Circuit was asked to decide whether to follow the
Ninth Circuit's approach in Ibarra or the Fifth Circuit's contrary approach in Prentiss
v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994) (fictional).

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether to follow the Ninth Circuit's approach in Ibarra, which applies Whitfield and
Calloway to uphold individualized race-conscious review, or the Fifth Circuit's approach
in Prentiss, which declines to follow Whitfield altogether.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel acknowledges that it sits as an intermediate appellate court bound in the
ordinary course to follow Supreme Court precedent such as Whitfield and Calloway, but,
like the Fifth Circuit in Prentiss, concludes that intervening doctrinal developments in
the Supreme Court's broader equal protection jurisprudence justify departing from
Whitfield's diversity rationale.

The panel finds Prentiss's reasoning -- that the diversity rationale lacks a logical
stopping point and cannot be measured with the objectivity strict scrutiny demands --
persuasive, and adopts it. The panel further agrees with Prentiss that even a genuinely
individualized process cannot cure what the panel views as the underlying problem: any
consideration of an applicant's race, however embedded in a larger holistic review, is a
racial classification subject to the Equal Protection Clause's most exacting scrutiny,
which the panel concludes the diversity rationale cannot survive.

The panel expressly declines to follow Ibarra, and acknowledges that its decision
deepens a circuit split now spanning the Fifth, Sixth, and Ninth Circuits, with the
Fifth and Sixth Circuits on one side and the Ninth Circuit -- along with the reasoning
underlying the Third Circuit's decision in Delgado and the district court's decision in
Brennan City -- on the other.

The panel states that it believes only the Supreme Court can resolve the growing
disagreement among the circuits over Whitfield's continued vitality, and it invites
further review.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994) -- this opinion follows that authority. Adopts the fifth circuit's reasoning declining to follow whitfield.
* Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Declines to follow the ninth circuit's approach in ibarra.
* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Declines to apply calloway's narrow-tailoring test as controlling.

Holding
-------

The Sixth Circuit sides with the Fifth Circuit's approach in Prentiss, declining to
follow Whitfield's diversity rationale and deepening the existing circuit split.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is REVERSED.
