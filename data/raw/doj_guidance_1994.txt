Background
----------

Following the Fifth Circuit's decision in Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29
F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994) (fictional), and continuing disagreement among the courts of
appeals over Whitfield's diversity rationale, the Department of Justice issued this
guidance to assist institutions navigating the resulting uncertainty.

Purpose of This Guidance
------------------------

How should institutions in circuits that have not yet addressed the Prentiss/Ibarra
disagreement structure their admissions practices pending Supreme Court resolution?

Guidance
--------

This guidance explains that a growing disagreement exists among the federal courts of
appeals: the Ninth Circuit, in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, has applied Whitfield
and Calloway to uphold individualized, race-conscious admissions review, while the Fifth
Circuit, in Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, has declined to follow Whitfield
altogether.

Pending resolution of this disagreement, the Department recommends that institutions
outside the Fifth Circuit continue to structure admissions consistent with Whitfield and
Calloway, using the three-part narrow-tailoring framework and maintaining documentation
of individualized review, periodic reassessment, and the absence of any fixed numerical
target.

The Department notes that institutions within the Fifth Circuit's jurisdiction are bound
by Prentiss and should consult counsel regarding admissions practices consistent with
that decision.

The Department expresses its view that this disagreement among the circuits is likely to
warrant Supreme Court review in the coming years and will update this guidance following
any such review.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994) -- this opinion interprets that authority. Summarizes the fifth circuit's departure from whitfield.
* Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989) -- this opinion interprets that authority. Summarizes the ninth circuit's application of whitfield and calloway.

Summary
-------

This guidance will remain in effect until superseded by further judicial or agency
