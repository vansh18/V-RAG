HARMON V. BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Harmon, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Trustees of Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 601 U.S. 155 (2023)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2023-06-29
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Chief Justice Rosalind Delacombe
Status: active
Topic: Overruling of Diversity Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

This companion case was brought against Milford University, a private institution
receiving federal funds and therefore subject to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of
1964, which this Court has long construed to impose obligations coextensive with the
Equal Protection Clause. The lower courts upheld Milford's revised admissions process,
and this Court granted certiorari together with Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth
University.

Facts
-----

Milford University, following the district court's injunction in Odom v. Milford
University, revised its essay-reading practice to integrate race-related content into
individualized file review rather than applying a uniform favorable adjustment. The
parties do not dispute that this revised process, if Reyes and Voss remain good law,
would satisfy narrow tailoring.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which applies to private universities
receiving federal funds, imposes the same prohibition on race-conscious admissions the
Court announces today under the Equal Protection Clause in Harmon v. Board of Trustees
of Ashworth University.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Opinion of the Court
--------------------

The Court reiterates its longstanding construction of Title VI as prohibiting the same
conduct the Equal Protection Clause prohibits when engaged in by a state actor, a
construction reaching back to this Court's earliest cases applying Title VI to private
universities receiving federal funds, including federal financial aid received by their
students.

Applying the reasoning set out at length in Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth
University, decided today, the Court holds that Milford University's revised admissions
process -- however genuinely integrated into individualized file review, and however
distinguishable from the uniform essay adjustment enjoined in Odom v. Milford University
-- still considers an individual applicant's race, and therefore cannot survive the rule
announced in Harmon.

The Court declines Milford's invitation to treat private universities differently from
public universities under Title VI, reasoning that Title VI's text and this Court's
consistent prior construction of it leave no room for a private-university exception to
the rule announced today.

The Court notes that its holding resolves the underlying question left open by the
district court's injunction in Odom: an essay-reading practice, even one genuinely
integrated into individualized review rather than applied as a uniform adjustment,
remains unlawful to the extent it considers an applicant's race as such, though,
consistent with Harmon, institutions may still consider an applicant's discussion of how
race has affected the applicant's own life.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion follows that authority. Extends the reasoning and holding of the companion harmon decision to private universities under title vi.
* Odom v. Milford University, 404 F. Supp. 3d 812 (D. Fentress 2019) -- this opinion overrules that authority. Resolves and supersedes the narrower question left open by the injunction in odom.
* Voss v. Milford University, 539 U.S. 344 (2003) -- this opinion overrules that authority. Overrules voss as to this same institution.

Holding
-------

The Court holds that Title VI imposes obligations coextensive with the Equal Protection
Clause and that the holding of Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University
therefore applies with equal force to private universities receiving federal funds.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the lower court is REVERSED.
