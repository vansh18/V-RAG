WHITCOMBE V. HARWICK COLLEGE
Whitcombe, Plaintiff,
v.
Harwick College, Defendant.

Citation: 481 F.3d 950 (8th Cir. 2007)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit
Decision Date: 2007-11-30
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Desmond Achterberg
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Ferreira v. Ashworth University, 779 F.3d 240 (2d Cir. 2015)
Topic: Insufficient Individualization

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Ellery Whitcombe sued Harwick College, challenging its admissions process as
insufficiently individualized under Reyes and Voss. The district court upheld the
process, and Whitcombe appealed to the Eighth Circuit.

Facts
-----

Harwick College's admissions office asserts that it pursues a qualitative critical mass
consistent with Reyes, but unlike Brennan City University in Delacroix, Harwick's
admissions files contain no narrative documentation explaining how any individual
applicant's race was weighed alongside other factors. Admissions officers testified only
in general terms about their practices, without file-specific examples.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an institution's general assertion that it conducts individualized review,
unsupported by file-specific documentation, satisfies Reyes and Voss.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel contrasts this case with the Sixth Circuit's decision in Delacroix v. Brennan
City University, where file-specific narrative documentation allowed the court to verify
that race was considered individually for each applicant rather than as a background
aggregate consideration.

Here, the panel finds the record contains only generalized testimony describing
Harwick's process in the abstract, with no evidence -- of the kind credited in Delacroix
-- showing how any particular applicant's file was actually evaluated. The panel reasons
that Reyes and Voss together require more than an institution's assurance that its
process is individualized; they require evidence that it is.

The panel is careful not to require a rigid documentation format, acknowledging that
Reyes did not itself impose a specific record-keeping requirement, but holds that some
file-specific evidence -- whether narrative notes, contemporaneous file annotations, or
other comparable documentation -- is necessary to permit meaningful judicial review of
whether a process is genuinely individualized.

The panel remands for further proceedings, suggesting that Harwick may be able to
develop an adequate record on remand but that the present record does not support
summary judgment in the College's favor.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Delacroix v. Brennan City University, 398 F.3d 611 (6th Cir. 2005) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes delacroix's file-specific documentation from harwick's generalized assertions.
* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies reyes's individualized-review requirement.
* Voss v. Milford University, 539 U.S. 344 (2003) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies voss's requirement of genuine, verifiable individualized assessment.

Holding
-------

The Eighth Circuit holds that Harwick's process is insufficiently individualized because
the record contains no file-specific evidence of how race was actually weighed for any
applicant, distinguishing Delacroix on that basis.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is VACATED and the case is REMANDED for further
proceedings.
