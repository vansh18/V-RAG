PARK V. CALLOWAY STATE COLLEGE
Park, Plaintiff,
v.
Calloway State College, Defendant.

Citation: 712 F. Supp. 3d 90 (D. Fentress 2025)
Court: United States District Court for the District of Fentress
Decision Date: 2025-02-19
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Priyanka Deshmukh
Status: active
Topic: Race-Neutral Alternatives

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Simone Park -- no relation to the plaintiff in Park v. Trent Institute of
Technology -- sued Calloway State College, alleging that its new socioeconomic-status-
based preference was in substance a disguised racial classification because the
College's internal planning documents referenced an expectation that the preference
would help maintain racial diversity.

Facts
-----

After Harmon, Calloway State College eliminated the point-bonus admissions index at
issue decades earlier in Nakamura v. Calloway State College and replaced it with a
preference based on applicants' socioeconomic status, measured by household income,
parental educational attainment, and neighborhood-level economic indicators, without
regard to race. Internal planning documents, produced in discovery, showed College
officials had projected that the socioeconomic preference would likely have some
positive effect on racial diversity, though the preference itself does not reference
race in its criteria or operation.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a facially race-neutral, socioeconomic-status-based admissions preference
becomes an unlawful racial classification under Harmon merely because the institution
anticipated, and internally discussed, that the preference might have some incidental
effect on racial diversity.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The court reasons that Harmon's holding is addressed to the consideration of an
individual applicant's race, not to an institution's motive in adopting a facially race-
neutral policy. Because the socioeconomic preference at issue here does not reference
race in its criteria, is not administered with reference to any applicant's race, and
applies uniformly regardless of race, the court finds no racial classification for
strict scrutiny to address.

The court distinguishes this case from the essay-scoring rubric struck down in Nguyen v.
Trent Institute of Technology, where the challenged practice expressly triggered a
benefit based on the presence of race-referencing content. Here, by contrast, no aspect
of the socioeconomic preference's operation depends on any applicant's race.

The court acknowledges College officials' internal discussion of the preference's likely
effect on racial diversity but finds this evidence of institutional motive, standing
alone, insufficient to convert a facially race-neutral policy into a racial
classification, particularly where -- as here -- the policy also serves independent,
race-neutral objectives related to socioeconomic access to higher education.

The court notes that its holding leaves open the different question, not presented here,
of whether a facially race-neutral criterion adopted as a knowing and deliberate proxy
for race, with no independent justification, might be treated differently.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies harmon's prohibition to conduct that considers race itself, not facially race-neutral criteria.
* Nguyen v. Trent Institute of Technology, 98 F.4th 214 (4th Cir. 2024) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes the race-triggered essay bonus in nguyen from the facially race-neutral preference at issue here.
* Nakamura v. Calloway State College, 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Notes the college's elimination of the point-bonus index at issue in the earlier nakamura litigation.

Holding
-------

The district court holds that a facially race-neutral preference does not become an
unlawful racial classification under Harmon merely because the institution anticipated
or hoped for an incidental diversity effect, so long as race itself plays no role in the
preference's criteria or administration.

Conclusion
----------

The court enters judgment for Calloway State College.
