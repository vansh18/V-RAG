PRENTISS V. TRENT INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY
Prentiss, Plaintiff,
v.
Trent Institute of Technology, Defendant.

Citation: 29 F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit
Decision Date: 1994-07-08
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Foster Langtry
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Topic: Rejection of Diversity Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Gregory Prentiss sued Trent Institute of Technology in federal district court,
challenging the Institute's revised, post-Delgado holistic admissions process as
inconsistent with the Equal Protection Clause. The district court, applying Calloway v.
Ashworth State University, upheld the process. Prentiss appealed to the Fifth Circuit.

Facts
-----

Following Delgado, Trent Institute of Technology abandoned its fixed set-aside and
adopted a holistic process similar to those upheld in Brennan City, Calloway, and
Ibarra. Prentiss did not dispute that the process was individualized, unweighted by a
fixed numerical target, and subject to periodic review; instead, he argued that
Whitfield and Calloway were themselves wrongly decided and should not be followed.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Whitfield's diversity rationale and Calloway's narrow-tailoring test should
continue to be followed, or whether the Equal Protection Clause forbids any
consideration of race in admissions regardless of how individualized the process may be.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel acknowledges that Whitfield and Calloway remain controlling authority from the
Supreme Court, but reasons that subsequent developments in this Court's equal protection
jurisprudence in other contexts -- which the panel reads to require a genuinely
compelling, non-diffuse interest before any racial classification will be sustained --
have eroded Whitfield's foundation to the point that the panel is prepared to depart
from it.

The panel concludes that the educational benefits of diversity, however real, are not
the kind of compelling interest capable of justifying a racial classification, because
unlike remediation of specific identified discrimination, the diversity rationale has no
logical stopping point and no objective means of measurement: no court or institution
can say with precision how much diversity is enough, or when the compelling interest has
been satisfied.

The panel further reasons that even a genuinely individualized process, of the kind
Trent Institute now uses, cannot be squared with the Equal Protection Clause's command
that individuals be treated as individuals, because any consideration of race, however
modest or however embedded within a larger holistic review, necessarily treats the
applicant differently because of race.

The panel expressly acknowledges that its holding conflicts with the Ninth Circuit's
decision in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law and effectively narrows Calloway to its
facts, and states that it believes the question warrants resolution by the Supreme
Court.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Declines to follow whitfield's diversity rationale.
* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Narrows calloway's narrow-tailoring test to its facts.
* Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Expressly creates a circuit split with the ninth circuit's decision in ibarra.
* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion follows that authority. Adopts the colorblind-constitution reasoning of the whitfield dissent.

Holding
-------

The Fifth Circuit holds that it will no longer follow Whitfield's diversity rationale,
reasoning that educational diversity is not a compelling interest capable of justifying
any consideration of race in admissions, and that race may never be a factor in
admissions decisions by a public institution.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is REVERSED, and the case is REMANDED with
instructions to enter judgment for Prentiss.
