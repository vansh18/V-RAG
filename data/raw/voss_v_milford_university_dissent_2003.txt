VOSS V. MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Voss, Petitioner,
v.
Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 539 U.S. 344 (2003)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2003-06-23
Opinion Type: Dissent
Author: Justice Grant Halvorsen
Status: active
Topic: Mechanical Point Systems

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Bridget Voss, denied undergraduate admission to Milford University -- the
same institution whose graduate admissions practices were at issue three decades earlier
in Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University -- sued in federal district
court, challenging Milford's point-based undergraduate admissions index as inconsistent
with Calloway's narrow-tailoring test. The district court upheld the index, relying on
Nakamura v. Calloway State College, 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991) (fictional).
The Fourth Circuit affirmed. This Court granted certiorari together with Reyes v.
Trustees of Ashworth University to address point-based admissions systems directly.

Facts
-----

Milford University's undergraduate admissions office uses a 100-point index. Every
applicant from a designated minority group automatically receives twenty points toward
the index, regardless of any other characteristic of the applicant's file. By
comparison, a perfect score on the University's required personal essay -- assessed by a
faculty reader -- yields at most twelve points, and a valedictorian class rank yields
ten points. Because the University admits any applicant scoring above a numerical
cutoff, the twenty-point bonus was, for many applicants from designated groups,
sufficient by itself to cross the admission threshold regardless of other
qualifications.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a transparent, fixed-value point system is more or less defensible than the
opaque, qualitative critical-mass approach approved the same day in Reyes.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Dissenting Opinion
------------------

Justice Halvorsen, dissenting in Reyes on the ground that critical mass is an
unreviewable quota, argues the Court's decision here compounds that error by striking
down the one admissions method that makes the weight assigned to race fully transparent
and subject to precise judicial review.

He argues that Milford's index, whatever one thinks of the size of the twenty-point
bonus, at least allows a reviewing court to see exactly how much weight race receives
relative to every other factor -- a transparency the qualitative, holistic processes
approved in Reyes and Calloway conspicuously lack.

In Justice Halvorsen's view, the Court's decision today creates a perverse incentive:
institutions seeking to use race in admissions will now have every reason to obscure the
weight assigned to race within an opaque, difficult-to-review holistic process, rather
than disclosing it in the transparent, numerically precise form Milford used.

He would uphold Milford's index, reasoning that if any use of race in admissions is to
be permitted at all -- a premise he does not accept, for the reasons given in his Reyes
dissent -- a transparent point system is the more administrable and more honest of the
two approaches before the Court today.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Builds on the critique of opaque, unreviewable diversity rationales set out in the companion reyes dissent.
* Nakamura v. Calloway State College, 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Would preserve nakamura's approval of transparent, fixed-value point systems.

Holding
-------

Justice Halvorsen dissents, arguing that Milford's transparent point system is, if
anything, more defensible than the opaque critical-mass approach the Court approves the
same day in Reyes.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Halvorsen respectfully dissents.
