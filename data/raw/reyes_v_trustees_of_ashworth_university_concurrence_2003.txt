REYES V. TRUSTEES OF ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Reyes, Petitioner,
v.
Trustees of Ashworth University, Respondent.

Citation: 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2003-06-23
Opinion Type: Concurrence
Author: Justice Thaddeus Okonkwo-Lindqvist
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Sunset Expectation for Race-Conscious Review

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Marisol Reyes, denied admission to Ashworth University, sued in federal
district court, which upheld the University's admissions program under Calloway v.
Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) (fictional). The Fourth Circuit affirmed.
This Court granted certiorari to resolve the disagreement among the courts of appeals
reflected in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989)
(fictional), on one hand, and Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771
(5th Cir. 1994) (fictional), and Nakamura v. Westbrook College of Law, 121 F.3d 355 (6th
Cir. 1997) (fictional), on the other.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University's admissions office reviews each application individually,
considering race as one factor among many with the stated goal of enrolling a 'critical
mass' of students from underrepresented groups. University officials testified that
critical mass is assessed qualitatively, by reference to whether students from
underrepresented groups report feeling able to participate in campus discourse without
serving as a token representative of their group, rather than by reference to any fixed
percentage or number.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the majority should signal an expected endpoint for race-conscious admissions
review.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Concurring Opinion
------------------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist joins the Court's opinion in full but writes separately to
underscore what he views as an important, if implicit, premise of the majority's
holding: that race-conscious admissions review is a time-bound remedy for present
conditions, not a permanent feature of American higher education.

He observes that the educational and social conditions that justify considering race in
admissions -- including continuing disparities in access to the kind of secondary
education that prepares students for competitive university admission -- are, in his
view, capable of meaningful improvement over time, and that institutions and courts
alike should treat today's holding as provisional rather than permanent.

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist expresses the expectation that, within approximately twenty-
five years, the use of race in admissions will no longer be necessary to achieve the
educational benefits of diversity, and he urges institutions to use the intervening
period to develop and refine race-neutral alternatives capable of achieving the same
objectives.

He closes by noting that this expectation is not a holding and does not bind future
courts, but reflects his considered judgment about the trajectory the Court's diversity
rationale should follow.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Joins the majority opinion in full.

Holding
-------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist concurs in full, writing separately to express his expectation
that race-conscious admissions review will no longer be necessary within a generation.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist concurs.
