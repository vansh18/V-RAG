HARMON V. BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Harmon, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Trustees of Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 601 U.S. 155 (2023)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2023-06-29
Opinion Type: Dissent
Author: Justice Thaddeus Okonkwo-Lindqvist
Status: active
Topic: Overruling of Diversity Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

This companion case was brought against Milford University, a private institution
receiving federal funds and therefore subject to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of
1964, which this Court has long construed to impose obligations coextensive with the
Equal Protection Clause. The lower courts upheld Milford's revised admissions process,
and this Court granted certiorari together with Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth
University.

Facts
-----

Milford University, following the district court's injunction in Odom v. Milford
University, revised its essay-reading practice to integrate race-related content into
individualized file review rather than applying a uniform favorable adjustment. The
parties do not dispute that this revised process, if Reyes and Voss remain good law,
would satisfy narrow tailoring.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Milford's revised, genuinely individualized essay-reading practice should be
treated differently from the uniform adjustment struck down in Odom, given the
majority's holding in the companion case.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Dissenting Opinion
------------------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist incorporates by reference the reasoning of his dissent in the
companion case decided today, Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, and
dissents here for the same reasons.

He adds that Milford's revised essay-reading practice was developed specifically to
comply with the district court's injunction in Odom v. Milford University, and finds it
notable that the very process a lower court required as a remedy for insufficient
individualization is today held unlawful for considering race at all, a shift he views
as illustrating how abruptly the majority's new rule departs from the incremental
doctrine that preceded it.

He would affirm the judgment below and allow Milford's revised practice, developed in
direct response to judicial guidance, to remain in effect.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Incorporates by reference the reasoning of the companion harmon dissent.
* Odom v. Milford University, 404 F. Supp. 3d 812 (D. Fentress 2019) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Notes that milford's revised practice complied with odom's requirements.

Holding
-------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist dissents for the reasons given in his companion dissent in
Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, and separately notes that Milford's
revised practice was a good-faith attempt to comply with the very standard Odom
articulated.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist respectfully dissents.
