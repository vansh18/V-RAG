REYES V. TRUSTEES OF ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Reyes, Petitioner,
v.
Trustees of Ashworth University, Respondent.

Citation: 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2003-06-23
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Chief Justice Priya Sundaram
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Individualized Holistic Review Standard

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Marisol Reyes, denied admission to Ashworth University, sued in federal
district court, which upheld the University's admissions program under Calloway v.
Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) (fictional). The Fourth Circuit affirmed.
This Court granted certiorari to resolve the disagreement among the courts of appeals
reflected in Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989)
(fictional), on one hand, and Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771
(5th Cir. 1994) (fictional), and Nakamura v. Westbrook College of Law, 121 F.3d 355 (6th
Cir. 1997) (fictional), on the other.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University's admissions office reviews each application individually,
considering race as one factor among many with the stated goal of enrolling a 'critical
mass' of students from underrepresented groups. University officials testified that
critical mass is assessed qualitatively, by reference to whether students from
underrepresented groups report feeling able to participate in campus discourse without
serving as a token representative of their group, rather than by reference to any fixed
percentage or number.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Whitfield's diversity rationale remains valid, and whether a university's
pursuit of a qualitatively defined 'critical mass' of underrepresented students
satisfies narrow tailoring under Calloway.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Opinion of the Court
--------------------

The Court begins by resolving the disagreement among the circuits. It disapproves the
Fifth and Sixth Circuits' conclusion in Prentiss and Nakamura v. Westbrook College of
Law that the diversity rationale cannot qualify as a compelling interest. In the Court's
view, the educational benefits that flow from a diverse student body -- including the
promotion of cross-racial understanding, the breakdown of racial stereotypes, and the
preparation of students for an increasingly diverse workforce and society -- are
substantial and are not the sort of interest this Court's strict scrutiny doctrine
requires courts to disregard merely because they resist precise numerical measurement.

The Court reaffirms Calloway's three-part narrow-tailoring test and clarifies its
application to the 'critical mass' concept at issue here. The Court holds that a
university may pursue a critical mass of underrepresented students, understood
qualitatively as the number sufficient to avoid tokenism and to realize the educational
benefits diversity is meant to provide, without thereby operating a quota, so long as
critical mass is assessed through ongoing, individualized review of the entire class
rather than through a fixed numerical target for any single group.

The Court is careful to distinguish this qualitative approach from the fixed set-aside
condemned in Delgado and from any approach that would assign race a predetermined,
mechanical weight without regard to the rest of an applicant's file -- a distinction
developed further in the Court's companion decision today in Voss v. Milford University.

The Court finds Ashworth University's practice of assessing critical mass through
qualitative indicators -- rather than through any fixed percentage or count --
consistent with individualized review, and credits testimony describing how admissions
officers read each file as a whole, with race remaining one consideration among many
including academic achievement, personal background, leadership, and geographic origin.

The Court reiterates Whitfield's expectation that race-conscious admissions programs are
a temporary, not a permanent, feature of higher education, and states its own
expectation -- elaborated further in the concurrence -- that the use of race in
admissions should become unnecessary as the educational and social conditions that
justify it continue to improve.

The Court also rejects Reyes's argument that any use of race, however individualized, is
inherently a covert quota because it is aimed, in the aggregate, at increasing minority
enrollment. The Court explains that an interest in the educational benefits of diversity
necessarily contemplates enrolling a diverse class in fact, and that this aggregate goal
does not convert an individualized process into a quota so long as no fixed number or
percentage constrains any individual admissions decision.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Reaffirms whitfield's diversity rationale as a compelling interest.
* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Reaffirms and elaborates calloway's three-part narrow-tailoring test.
* Prentiss v. Trent Institute of Technology, 29 F.3d 771 (5th Cir. 1994) -- this opinion overrules that authority. Expressly disapproves the fifth circuit's rejection of the diversity rationale.
* Nakamura v. Westbrook College of Law, 121 F.3d 355 (6th Cir. 1997) -- this opinion overrules that authority. Expressly disapproves the sixth circuit's rejection of the diversity rationale.
* Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Approves the ninth circuit's application of whitfield and calloway.
* Delgado v. Trent Institute of Technology, 538 F.2d 902 (3d Cir. 1976) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Reaffirms delgado's prohibition on fixed set-asides.

Holding
-------

The Court reaffirms Whitfield and Calloway, disapproves Prentiss and Nakamura v.
Westbrook College of Law, and holds that the pursuit of a qualitatively defined critical
mass, assessed through individualized review rather than fixed numerical targets,
satisfies narrow tailoring.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the Fourth Circuit is AFFIRMED.
