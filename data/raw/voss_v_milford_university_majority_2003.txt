VOSS V. MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Voss, Petitioner,
v.
Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 539 U.S. 344 (2003)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 2003-06-23
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Chief Justice Priya Sundaram
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Mechanical Point Systems

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Bridget Voss, denied undergraduate admission to Milford University -- the
same institution whose graduate admissions practices were at issue three decades earlier
in Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University -- sued in federal district
court, challenging Milford's point-based undergraduate admissions index as inconsistent
with Calloway's narrow-tailoring test. The district court upheld the index, relying on
Nakamura v. Calloway State College, 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991) (fictional).
The Fourth Circuit affirmed. This Court granted certiorari together with Reyes v.
Trustees of Ashworth University to address point-based admissions systems directly.

Facts
-----

Milford University's undergraduate admissions office uses a 100-point index. Every
applicant from a designated minority group automatically receives twenty points toward
the index, regardless of any other characteristic of the applicant's file. By
comparison, a perfect score on the University's required personal essay -- assessed by a
faculty reader -- yields at most twelve points, and a valedictorian class rank yields
ten points. Because the University admits any applicant scoring above a numerical
cutoff, the twenty-point bonus was, for many applicants from designated groups,
sufficient by itself to cross the admission threshold regardless of other
qualifications.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an admissions index that automatically awards a fixed, outsized point bonus for
membership in a designated minority group -- without regard to any other aspect of the
applicant's file -- satisfies narrow tailoring under Calloway.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Opinion of the Court
--------------------

The Court distinguishes Ashworth University's critical-mass approach, approved today in
Reyes, from Milford's point-bonus index. In Reyes, the Court explains, race is one
qualitative consideration weighed alongside every other aspect of an applicant's file as
part of a genuinely individualized process. Here, by contrast, the automatic twenty-
point bonus is awarded based on group membership alone, without any accompanying
assessment of how that applicant's background, considered individually, might contribute
to the University's diversity objectives.

The Court finds it particularly significant that the twenty-point bonus exceeds the
maximum points available for any other single factor, including the University's own
assessment of an applicant's personal essay -- a factor specifically designed to capture
individualized qualities. The Court reasons that assigning race a larger, guaranteed
bonus than any individualized assessment can yield effectively makes race the decisive
factor for many applicants, contrary to Whitfield's requirement that race be one factor
among many rather than a determinative one.

The Court rejects Milford's argument, accepted by the district court below in reliance
on Nakamura v. Calloway State College, that a point system is permissible so long as
every factor receives some fixed numerical value. The Court explains that Nakamura's
reasoning failed to grapple with the practical effect of a bonus large enough to be
outcome-determinative for a substantial number of applicants, and to that extent
Nakamura is overruled.

The Court emphasizes that its holding does not forbid all numerical scoring in
admissions; it forbids only a system in which the racial bonus is large enough, and
automatic enough, to substitute for individualized review of the rest of the applicant's
file. A point system that assigns a modest, non-determinative value to race, embedded
within a broader individualized review of each file, may well satisfy narrow tailoring,
but the mechanical, outcome-determinative index here does not.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies calloway's individualized-review prong to strike down a mechanical index.
* Nakamura v. Calloway State College, 779 F. Supp. 2d 1042 (D. Fentress 1991) -- this opinion overrules that authority. Overrules nakamura's approval of fixed-value point systems to the extent inconsistent with today's holding.
* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes ashworth's qualitative critical-mass approach, approved today in the companion case, from milford's mechanical point bonus.

Holding
-------

The Court holds that Milford's twenty-point automatic racial bonus is not narrowly
tailored because it makes race a decisive factor for a significant number of applicants
without individualized consideration of the rest of each applicant's file, and overrules
Nakamura v. Calloway State College to the extent it is inconsistent with today's
holding.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the Fourth Circuit is REVERSED.
