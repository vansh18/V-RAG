MARSH V. BOARD OF REGENTS OF CALLOWAY STATE COLLEGE
Marsh, Plaintiff,
v.
Board of Regents of Calloway State College, Defendant.

Citation: 412 F.2d 118 (4th Cir. 1971)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit
Decision Date: 1971-05-14
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Adelaide Prewitt
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974)
Topic: Standard of Review

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Warren Marsh, a rejected applicant, sued the Board of Regents of Calloway
State College in the United States District Court for the District of Fentress, alleging
that the College's admissions preference violated the Equal Protection Clause of the
Fourteenth Amendment. The district court granted summary judgment for the College,
applying rational-basis review and concluding that the preference was rationally related
to the College's interest in expanding educational opportunity. Marsh appealed.

Facts
-----

Calloway State College adopted an admissions preference in 1968 under which applicants
from designated minority groups received additional consideration during file review.
The College's stated purpose was to expand access to higher education for groups it
found to be underrepresented in its student body. Marsh, who was not a member of a
designated group, was denied admission in the 1970 entering class despite academic
credentials the district court found comparable to those of several admitted minority
applicants.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a state college's race-conscious admissions preference is subject to rational-
basis review or a more searching form of judicial scrutiny under the Equal Protection
Clause.

Standard of Review
------------------

The court below applied rational-basis review, under which a classification is sustained
if it is rationally related to a legitimate governmental interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel begins from the premise that not every racial classification triggers the same
order of judicial concern. In the panel's view, a classification that opens doors rather
than closes them -- one that extends opportunity to a group rather than excluding a
group -- occupies a different constitutional position than the invidious classifications
this Court's most demanding equal protection cases have condemned.

The panel finds it material that the College's preference was adopted against a
documented backdrop of exclusion: as recently as 1962, the College's own admissions
files reflect a formal policy of excluding applicants from the designated groups
altogether. The 1968 preference, the panel reasons, is best understood as a corrective
measure addressing that documented history, and corrective measures of this kind bear a
rational relationship to the College's legitimate interest in expanding access to public
higher education.

The panel declines to adopt the more searching standard Marsh urges, reasoning that
heightened scrutiny in this context risks treating a remedial preference and an
exclusionary quota as constitutional equivalents, when the panel views them as
opposites.

The panel further notes, without extended discussion, that the College's preference does
not operate as an absolute bar to any applicant's admission and that Marsh's own file
was reviewed alongside those of all other applicants.

Holding
-------

The Fourth Circuit holds that because the College's preference is designed to remedy the
effects of historical exclusion and to expand, rather than restrict, educational
opportunity, it is subject only to rational-basis review, under which it is upheld.

Conclusion
----------

Because the College's admissions preference bears a rational relationship to its
legitimate interest in remedying the documented effects of past exclusion and expanding
access to public higher education, the judgment of the district court is AFFIRMED.
