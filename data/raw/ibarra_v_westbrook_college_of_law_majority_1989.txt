IBARRA V. WESTBROOK COLLEGE OF LAW
Ibarra, Plaintiff,
v.
Westbrook College of Law, Defendant.

Citation: 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
Decision Date: 1989-08-15
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Corinne Whitlark
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Topic: Application of Narrow Tailoring Test

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Teodoro Ibarra, denied admission to Westbrook College of Law, sued in federal
district court alleging Westbrook's holistic process failed the narrow-tailoring test
articulated in Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) (fictional).
The district court granted summary judgment for Westbrook. Ibarra appealed to the Ninth
Circuit.

Facts
-----

Westbrook College of Law's admissions committee reviews each file individually,
considering undergraduate record, admissions-examination score, personal statement, work
experience, and race as one of numerous factors. The College's dean testified that the
admissions committee conducts an annual review, modeled explicitly on the process
described in Calloway, to confirm that race is not functioning as a de facto quota.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Westbrook's admissions process satisfies the three-part narrow-tailoring test
articulated in Calloway.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel walks through each prong of the Calloway test in turn. On individualized
review, the panel credits testimony and documentary evidence describing a file-by-file
evaluation process in which no fixed weight is assigned to race, and in which committee
members expressly discuss each applicant's full profile before reaching a decision.

On the absence of a fixed numerical target, the panel finds no evidence that Westbrook
reserves any set number of seats for any racial group, and rejects Ibarra's argument
that a modest year-over-year increase in minority enrollment following the program's
adoption is itself evidence of a quota, reasoning that Calloway specifically rejected
statistical patterns as sufficient, standing alone, to establish an unlawful quota.

On periodic reassessment, the panel finds Westbrook's annual review process -- modeled
on the very audit Calloway credited -- sufficient to satisfy the third prong, and
commends the College for building Calloway's requirement directly into its admissions
governance structure.

The panel closes by noting that Westbrook's practices offer other institutions a useful
template for satisfying Calloway going forward, particularly the practice of documenting
individualized review contemporaneously rather than reconstructing it after litigation
is filed.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Calloway v. Ashworth State University, 476 U.S. 512 (1986) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies the three-part narrow-tailoring test to uphold westbrook's program.

Holding
-------

The Ninth Circuit holds that Westbrook's process satisfies all three prongs of the
Calloway test and affirms summary judgment for the College.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is AFFIRMED.
