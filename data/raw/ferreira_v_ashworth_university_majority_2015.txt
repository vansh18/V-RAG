FERREIRA V. ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Ferreira, Plaintiff,
v.
Ashworth University, Defendant.

Citation: 779 F.3d 240 (2d Cir. 2015)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit
Decision Date: 2015-04-09
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Aline Ferreira-Boudreaux
Status: overruled
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Workability of Race-Neutral Alternatives

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Antonio Ferreira sued Ashworth University -- the same institution whose
admissions process this Court approved in Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University
twelve years earlier -- in the United States District Court for the District of
Fentress, arguing that Ashworth had not seriously considered whether race-neutral
alternatives could achieve its diversity objectives, as Justice Okonkwo-Lindqvist's
Reyes concurrence anticipated institutions would eventually need to demonstrate. The
district court upheld the program. The Second Circuit reviewed the appeal.

Facts
-----

By 2015, a number of other institutions -- though not Ashworth University -- had piloted
race-neutral admissions alternatives, including socioeconomic-status-based preferences
and percentage plans guaranteeing admission to top-ranked students from every high
school in the state. Ashworth's own record contained no evidence that it had studied or
piloted any such alternative; University officials testified only that they believed
race-neutral alternatives would be less effective without having tested that belief
against Ashworth's own applicant pool.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an institution invoking the diversity rationale must make an evidence-based
showing, specific to its own applicant pool, that race-neutral alternatives are
unworkable, or whether a good-faith belief that such alternatives would be less
effective suffices.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel reads Reyes and Calloway together to impose a genuine workability requirement,
not a formality: an institution must show that it seriously considered race-neutral
alternatives and reasonably concluded, based on evidence rather than assumption, that
they would not achieve the institution's diversity objectives about as well and at a
tolerable administrative cost.

The panel finds Ashworth's showing deficient because it rests entirely on officials'
good-faith beliefs rather than on any study or pilot program specific to Ashworth's own
applicant pool, even though other institutions had by 2015 developed a substantial body
of experience with race-neutral alternatives that Ashworth could have used to inform, or
could have replicated in, its own analysis.

The panel is careful to note that it does not require an institution to actually adopt
and fail with a race-neutral alternative before turning to race-conscious review -- a
requirement it acknowledges could itself harm the very students race-conscious review is
meant to benefit -- but does require some evidence-based analysis beyond good-faith
assertion.

The panel remands for Ashworth to develop an evidentiary record regarding the
workability of race-neutral alternatives, explaining that this requirement, while more
demanding than the documentation-focused inquiry of Delacroix and Whitcombe, is
consistent with and elaborates upon Reyes's narrow-tailoring framework rather than
departing from it.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion narrows that authority. Narrows reyes by requiring an evidence-based workability showing rather than a good-faith assertion.
* Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003) -- this opinion follows that authority. Builds on justice okonkwo-lindqvist's expectation that institutions would develop race-neutral alternatives over time.
* Delacroix v. Brennan City University, 398 F.3d 611 (6th Cir. 2005) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes delacroix's documentation-focused inquiry from the workability showing required here.

Holding
-------

The Second Circuit holds that narrow tailoring requires an institution to make a
rigorous, evidence-based showing -- through actual study or piloting specific to its own
applicant pool -- that workable race-neutral alternatives cannot achieve its diversity
objectives, and that a good-faith but untested belief is insufficient.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is VACATED and the case is REMANDED for further
proceedings.
