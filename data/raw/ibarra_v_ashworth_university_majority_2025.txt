IBARRA V. ASHWORTH UNIVERSITY
Ibarra, Plaintiff,
v.
Ashworth University, Defendant.

Citation: 730 F. Supp. 3d 501 (D. Fentress 2025)
Court: United States District Court for the District of Fentress
Decision Date: 2025-07-08
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Owen Pryce
Status: active
Topic: Legacy Admissions as Race Proxy

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Consuela Ibarra -- no relation to the plaintiff in Ibarra v. Westbrook College
of Law -- sued Ashworth University, alleging that its longstanding legacy and major-
donor admissions preferences function as a proxy for race because, as a statistical
matter, applicants who benefit from those preferences are disproportionately white, and
that Harmon's elimination of affirmative race-conscious review makes the racially skewed
effect of these facially race-neutral preferences newly untenable.

Facts
-----

Ashworth University has maintained legacy and major-donor admissions preferences for
decades. Statistical evidence in the record shows that applicants who benefit from these
preferences are disproportionately white relative to the overall applicant pool. The
preferences do not reference race in their criteria and are administered without regard
to any applicant's race. This is the first case in the corpus to test whether such a
facially race-neutral preference, with a documented disparate statistical effect, is
unlawful after Harmon.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether a facially race-neutral admissions preference with a documented disparate racial
effect, but no explicit consideration of race in its criteria or administration,
violates the Equal Protection Clause or Title VI after Harmon.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The court notes that Harmon addressed the direct consideration of an individual
applicant's race and did not address facially race-neutral criteria with a disparate
statistical effect, a distinct doctrinal question this Court's broader equal protection
case law has traditionally analyzed under a discriminatory-intent framework rather than
under strict scrutiny's compelling-interest framework.

Applying that intent-focused framework, the court finds the record insufficient to show
that Ashworth's legacy and donor preferences were adopted, or are currently
administered, with the purpose of favoring applicants because of race, as distinct from
the purpose -- which the record does support -- of favoring applicants with a prior
institutional or financial connection to the University.

The court expressly declines to hold that a disparate statistical effect alone, without
evidence of discriminatory purpose, is sufficient to invalidate a facially race-neutral
preference, but acknowledges that Park v. Calloway State College left open exactly this
question -- whether a knowing and deliberate use of a facially neutral criterion as a
proxy for race might be treated differently -- and finds this record does not present a
clear example of such deliberate proxy use.

The court acknowledges the significant public debate, reflected in the Department of
Education's contemporaneous guidance process, over whether legacy and donor preferences
should be reconsidered on policy grounds regardless of their constitutional status, but
emphasizes that policy judgments of that kind are for the University and, where
applicable, the political branches, not the courts, to make.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes harmon's direct-consideration holding from the disparate-effect theory advanced here.
* Park v. Calloway State College, 712 F. Supp. 3d 90 (D. Fentress 2025) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Notes park's open question regarding deliberate proxy use, which this record does not present.
* Ibarra v. Westbrook College of Law, 891 F.2d 233 (9th Cir. 1989) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. No relation to the unconnected 1989 ibarra litigation.

Holding
-------

The district court declines to extend Harmon to reach facially race-neutral preferences
with a merely disparate statistical effect, absent evidence that the preference was
adopted or is administered as a deliberate proxy for race, but acknowledges substantial
uncertainty in how this question should be analyzed and does not purport to resolve it
definitively.

Conclusion
----------

The court enters judgment for Ashworth University, without prejudice to a future case
presenting stronger evidence of deliberate proxy use.
