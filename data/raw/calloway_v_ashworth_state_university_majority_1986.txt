CALLOWAY V. ASHWORTH STATE UNIVERSITY
Calloway, Petitioner,
v.
Ashworth State University, Respondent.

Citation: 476 U.S. 512 (1986)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 1986-06-24
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Justice Desmond Ruiz
Status: limited
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Narrow Tailoring Test

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Daniel Calloway, a rejected applicant to Ashworth State University, sued in
the United States District Court for the District of Fentress, alleging that the
University's holistic admissions process nonetheless functioned as an informal quota
because the percentage of minority students in each entering class had remained within a
narrow band for six consecutive years. The district court and the Fourth Circuit both
rejected the claim, applying Whitfield and Brennan City. This Court granted certiorari
to clarify the narrow-tailoring inquiry left open in Whitfield.

Facts
-----

Ashworth State University's admissions office reviews each application individually and
considers race as one of many factors. Over six consecutive admissions cycles, the
percentage of the entering class identifying as a member of a designated minority group
ranged between eleven and thirteen percent. Calloway argued this statistical stability
was itself evidence of an unwritten quota functioning beneath a facially individualized
process.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

What showing satisfies the narrow-tailoring requirement left open in Whitfield, and does
year-over-year statistical stability in the racial composition of an entering class,
standing alone, establish an unlawful quota?

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Opinion of the Court
--------------------

The Court uses this case to articulate, for the first time in a single holding, the
narrow-tailoring framework implicit in Whitfield and applied piecemeal in the lower
courts in Delgado and Brennan City. A race-conscious admissions program is narrowly
tailored only if: first, race is considered as part of a truly individualized review of
the whole applicant, not assigned a fixed or predetermined weight; second, the program
does not operate, whether formally or informally, as a quota reserving a set number or
percentage of seats for any group; and third, the institution periodically reassesses
whether continued consideration of race remains necessary to achieve its stated
educational objectives.

Applying that framework, the Court holds that statistical stability in the racial
composition of an entering class, without more, does not by itself establish a quota. A
truly individualized process can produce stable outcomes for reasons having nothing to
do with a hidden numerical target -- for example, stability in the qualifications and
interests of the broader applicant pool from year to year. The Court declines to adopt a
rule under which any statistical consistency automatically converts an otherwise
individualized process into an unlawful quota, reasoning that such a rule would
perversely penalize institutions for admissions outcomes they do not directly control.

The Court reaffirms Whitfield's diversity rationale over the narrower remedial rationale
urged in Justice Okafor's Whitfield concurrence, explaining that a university's interest
in the educational benefits of diversity does not depend on proof of the institution's
own specific history of discrimination, and that requiring such proof in every case
would leave many institutions unable to pursue diversity even where the educational
benefits are genuine and substantiated.

The Court emphasizes the third prong of its test -- periodic reassessment -- as a
meaningful, ongoing constraint, not a formality. Institutions relying on a diversity
rationale must be able to show that they have considered, and continue to consider,
whether race-neutral means could achieve the same educational objectives, and must be
prepared to discontinue race-conscious measures once they are no longer necessary.

On the record before it, the Court finds Ashworth State's annual internal audit process
-- which examines whether admissions outcomes reflect individualized review rather than
an emergent quota -- sufficient evidence of ongoing reassessment to satisfy the third
prong.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Reaffirms whitfield's diversity rationale as the operative compelling interest.
* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion declines to adopt the approach of that authority. Declines to require a remedial showing of institution-specific discrimination as urged in justice okafor's concurrence.
* Delgado v. Trent Institute of Technology, 538 F.2d 902 (3d Cir. 1976) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Affirms delgado's prohibition on fixed set-asides as consistent with the second prong of the narrow-tailoring test.
* In re Admissions Policy of Brennan City University, 312 F. Supp. 774 (E.D. Fentress 1978) -- this opinion affirms that authority. Affirms brennan city's approval of unified, individualized review.

Holding
-------

The Court adopts a three-part narrow-tailoring test -- individualized consideration of
every applicant, an absence of any fixed numerical target, and periodic reassessment of
the program's continued necessity -- and holds that statistical stability alone, without
more, does not establish an unlawful quota.

Conclusion
----------

Because Ashworth State University's admissions program satisfies all three prongs of the
narrow-tailoring test articulated today, the judgment of the Fourth Circuit is AFFIRMED.
