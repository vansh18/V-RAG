DELACROIX V. WESTBROOK COLLEGE OF LAW
Delacroix, Plaintiff,
v.
Westbrook College of Law, Defendant.

Citation: 101 F.4th 782 (9th Cir. 2024)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
Decision Date: 2024-09-12
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Corinne Whitlark
Status: active
Topic: Essay-Based Proxy Consideration

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Camille Delacroix -- no relation to the parties in Delacroix v. Brennan City
University -- sued Westbrook College of Law, alleging that its essay-reading guidance,
which instructs readers to consider race-related adversity narratives as part of
individualized character assessment, violates Harmon.

Facts
-----

Westbrook College of Law's post-Harmon essay guidance instructs readers to evaluate each
essay for what it reveals about the applicant's resilience, judgment, and individual
character, and specifically instructs readers that an applicant's discussion of
overcoming race-related adversity may be credited on the same basis as any other
adversity narrative -- financial hardship, family illness, or immigration status, for
example -- without any fixed point value assigned based on whether the essay references
race.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an essay-reading practice that credits race-related adversity narratives on the
same individualized basis as other adversity narratives, without a fixed race-triggered
point value, is consistent with Harmon.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel reads Harmon's essay clarification to permit exactly the kind of
individualized assessment Westbrook conducts: crediting an applicant's discussion of how
race affected the applicant's life on the same basis as any other individual experience,
without treating race as a distinct, separately weighted criterion.

The panel expressly disagrees with the Fourth Circuit's reasoning in Nguyen v. Trent
Institute of Technology, decided several months earlier, to the extent Nguyen can be
read to forbid any instruction directing readers to consider race-related adversity
narratives at all. The panel reads Harmon's essay clarification as affirmatively
contemplating that such narratives may be credited, not merely tolerating them as an
unavoidable byproduct of essay review.

The panel finds it dispositive that Westbrook's guidance, unlike the rubric struck down
in Nguyen, assigns no fixed point value tied to the presence of racial content, and
instead instructs readers to evaluate race-related adversity on the same individualized
footing as financial hardship, family illness, or immigration status -- categories the
panel notes even the Fourth Circuit's opinion in Nguyen did not suggest were
impermissible to credit.

The panel acknowledges that its holding creates a square conflict with Nguyen regarding
how Harmon's essay clarification applies to guidance-level instructions to admissions
readers, and states its view that further guidance from the Supreme Court may ultimately
be necessary to resolve the disagreement.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion follows that authority. Applies harmon's essay clarification to uphold individualized consideration of race-related adversity.
* Nguyen v. Trent Institute of Technology, 98 F.4th 214 (4th Cir. 2024) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Expressly disagrees with the fourth circuit's narrower reading of harmon's essay clarification, creating a new circuit split.

Holding
-------

The Ninth Circuit holds that Westbrook's practice is consistent with Harmon because it
evaluates race-related adversity narratives on the same individualized basis as any
other form of adversity, without treating the presence of racial content as itself
meaningful, and expressly disagrees with the Fourth Circuit's decision in Nguyen.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is AFFIRMED.
