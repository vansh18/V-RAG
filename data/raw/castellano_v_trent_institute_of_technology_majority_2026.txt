CASTELLANO V. TRENT INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY
Castellano, Plaintiff,
v.
Trent Institute of Technology, Defendant.

Citation: 110 F.4th 305 (5th Cir. 2026)
Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit
Decision Date: 2026-03-17
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Marcus Castellane
Status: active
Topic: Graduate Admissions Scope

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Plaintiff Renata Castellano -- no relation to Judge Marcus Castellane -- sued Trent
Institute of Technology's graduate engineering program, which had adopted essay-reading
guidance modeled explicitly on the Ninth Circuit's decision in Delacroix v. Westbrook
College of Law rather than the Fourth Circuit's decision in Nguyen v. Trent Institute of
Technology, which struck down the same University's undergraduate essay-scoring rubric
two years earlier.

Facts
-----

After the Fourth Circuit enjoined Trent Institute's undergraduate essay-scoring rubric
in Nguyen, the University's graduate engineering program -- located within the
geographic jurisdiction of the Fifth Circuit rather than the Fourth -- adopted guidance
modeled on the Ninth Circuit's approach in Delacroix v. Westbrook College of Law,
instructing readers to credit race-related adversity narratives on the same
individualized basis as other adversity narratives.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether Harmon applies with equal force to graduate and professional-school admissions,
and whether the Fifth Circuit should follow the Fourth Circuit's approach in Nguyen or
the Ninth Circuit's approach in Delacroix regarding essay-reading guidance.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The panel first holds, as a threshold matter not disputed by any party, that nothing in
Harmon's reasoning distinguishes graduate or professional admissions from undergraduate
admissions; the Equal Protection Clause's prohibition on considering an applicant's
race, the panel reasons, does not depend on the applicant's degree level.

Turning to the essay-reading question, the panel acknowledges the existing conflict
between the Fourth Circuit's decision in Nguyen and the Ninth Circuit's decision in
Delacroix, and elects to follow Nguyen. The panel reasons that any instruction directing
admissions readers to specifically credit race-related content -- even alongside other
forms of adversity, as in Delacroix -- risks reintroducing race as an operative factor
by directing readers' attention to racial identity in a way ordinary individualized
essay review would not.

The panel expresses concern that the Ninth Circuit's approach in Delacroix, by
explicitly instructing readers to consider race-related adversity narratives,
effectively reintroduces race consciousness through the essay-reading process, even
without a fixed point value, and finds this concern more consistent with Harmon's
reasoning than the Ninth Circuit's contrary conclusion.

The panel expressly acknowledges the resulting three-way disagreement in the circuits --
the Fourth and now Fifth Circuits on one side, the Ninth Circuit on the other -- and
states that it believes the question is likely to warrant further review.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion follows that authority. Extends harmon's holding to graduate and professional admissions.
* Nguyen v. Trent Institute of Technology, 98 F.4th 214 (4th Cir. 2024) -- this opinion follows that authority. Adopts the fourth circuit's narrower reading of harmon's essay clarification.
* Delacroix v. Westbrook College of Law, 101 F.4th 782 (9th Cir. 2024) -- this opinion criticizes that authority. Declines to follow the ninth circuit's broader reading of harmon's essay clarification, deepening the circuit split.

Holding
-------

The Fifth Circuit holds that Harmon applies with equal force to graduate and
professional admissions, and, addressing the essay-reading question directly, sides with
the Fourth Circuit's approach in Nguyen over the Ninth Circuit's approach in Delacroix,
further deepening the existing circuit split.

Conclusion
----------

The judgment of the district court is REVERSED.
