Background
----------

Following the Supreme Court's decisions in Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth
University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) (fictional), and Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Milford
University, 601 U.S. 155 (2023) (fictional), the Departments of Justice and Education
jointly issued this guidance to help institutions bring their admissions practices into
compliance and to rescind all prior guidance premised on Reyes and Voss.

Purpose of This Guidance
------------------------

How should institutions revise admissions practices developed under Reyes and Voss to
comply with Harmon, and what remains of the prior guidance framework?

Guidance
--------

This guidance formally rescinds the Departments' 2004 Joint Guidance and the Office for
Civil Rights' 2008 and 2016 guidance documents, all of which were premised on the
narrow-tailoring framework established in Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University and
Voss v. Milford University and now overruled by Harmon.

Consistent with Harmon, institutions may no longer consider an applicant's race, whether
through a numerical index, a qualitative critical-mass objective, or an essay-reading
practice that assigns weight based on an applicant's racial identity as such. This
prohibition applies regardless of how individualized or well-documented the process may
be; the file-specific documentation and evidentiary workability showings described in
the Departments' now-rescinded prior guidance no longer render a race-conscious practice
lawful.

Consistent with Harmon's clarification, institutions may continue to consider an
applicant's discussion, in an essay or elsewhere in the application, of how race has
affected the applicant's own life, so long as that consideration is treated as it would
be for any other aspect of the applicant's background and is not used as a proxy for the
applicant's race itself. The Departments acknowledge that applying this distinction in
practice may prove difficult, and institutions should consult counsel regarding specific
essay-reading and file-review practices.

Institutions remain free to pursue student-body diversity through race-neutral means,
including socioeconomic-status-based preferences, percentage plans, geographic and
first-generation-status criteria, and expanded recruitment and outreach, consistent with
the alternatives discussed in Harmon and previously piloted by some institutions under
Ferreira and Castellano.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023) -- this opinion interprets that authority. Explains and implements the harmon holding.
* Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Milford University, 601 U.S. 155 (2023) -- this opinion interprets that authority. Explains and implements the companion title vi holding.
* Joint Guidance of the Departments of Education and Justice on Race-Conscious Admissions After Reyes and Voss, DOE-DOJ Joint Guidance 2004-01 -- this opinion supersedes that authority. Formally rescinds the 2004 joint guidance.
* Office for Civil Rights Guidance on the Critical Mass Standard, OCR Memo 08-11 -- this opinion supersedes that authority. Formally rescinds the 2008 guidance.
* Office for Civil Rights Guidance Reaffirming the Critical Mass Framework, OCR Memo 16-09 -- this opinion supersedes that authority. Formally rescinds the 2016 guidance.

Summary
-------

This guidance is effective immediately and supersedes all prior guidance premised on
Reyes and Voss.
