WHITFIELD V. BOARD OF REGENTS OF MILFORD UNIVERSITY
Whitfield, Petitioner,
v.
Board of Regents of Milford University, Respondent.

Citation: 417 U.S. 226 (1974)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decision Date: 1974-06-10
Opinion Type: Concurrence
Author: Justice Ines Okafor
Status: limited
Overruled/Superseded By: Harmon v. Board of Trustees of Ashworth University, 601 U.S. 118 (2023)
Topic: Compelling Interest - Remedial Rationale

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

Petitioner Eleanor Whitfield, a rejected applicant to Milford University's undergraduate
program, filed suit in the United States District Court for the District of Fentress
alleging that the University's consideration of race in admissions violated the Equal
Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. The district court applied the rational-
basis standard set out in Marsh v. Board of Regents of Calloway State College, 412 F.2d
118 (4th Cir. 1971) (fictional), and entered judgment for the University. The Fourth
Circuit affirmed on the same rationale. This Court granted certiorari to resolve whether
race-conscious admissions programs in public higher education are subject to strict
scrutiny.

Facts
-----

Milford University's admissions office reviews each application individually and
considers an applicant's race as one factor among several in assembling an entering
class the University describes as diverse along multiple dimensions, including but not
limited to race. No fixed number of seats is set aside for any racial group, and race is
not assigned a predetermined numerical weight. Whitfield, who is white, was denied
admission in a year in which the University admitted a number of minority applicants
whose standardized test scores and grade point averages were, on the summary record
before the Court, lower than Whitfield's.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether the compelling interest supporting race-conscious admissions should rest on the
educational benefits of diversity, as the majority holds, or on the remediation of
specific, documented past discrimination.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Concurring Opinion
------------------

Justice Okafor agrees that strict scrutiny is the correct standard and agrees that the
case must be remanded, but writes separately because she would not locate the compelling
interest in the educational benefits of diversity, a rationale she views as diffuse,
difficult for courts to police, and untethered to any particular wrong.

In Justice Okafor's view, the more defensible and more limited constitutional basis for
race-conscious admissions is the remediation of specific, documented discrimination --
whether by the State, the institution itself, or society generally, where the
institution has been a willing participant in that discrimination. On the record here,
she notes, Milford University excluded applicants from certain minority groups by formal
policy as recently as 1961, a fact the majority does not discuss but which Justice
Okafor considers central to any compelling-interest analysis.

A remedial rationale, Justice Okafor writes, has the virtue of a natural stopping point:
once the documented effects of past exclusion have been addressed, the compelling
interest -- and with it, the constitutional justification for race-conscious measures --
expires. A diversity rationale untethered to any specific wrong, by contrast, risks
becoming a permanent feature of admissions decisionmaking, a result Justice Okafor
believes the Equal Protection Clause cannot tolerate.

Justice Okafor would remand with instructions that the lower courts develop a factual
record concerning Milford's own history of exclusion and assess narrow tailoring against
a remedial, not a diversity, rationale.

Holding
-------

Justice Okafor concurs in the judgment vacating and remanding but would ground any
compelling interest exclusively in the remediation of specific, documented
discrimination by the institution or the State, not in the abstract educational value of
diversity.

Conclusion
----------

Justice Okafor concurs in the judgment vacating and remanding the case, but on the
narrower, remedial rationale set out above.
