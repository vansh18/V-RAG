IN RE ADMISSIONS POLICY OF BRENNAN CITY UNIVERSITY
In re, Plaintiff,
v.
Admissions Policy of Brennan City University, Defendant.

Citation: 312 F. Supp. 774 (E.D. Fentress 1978)
Court: United States District Court for the Eastern District of Fentress
Decision Date: 1978-09-19
Opinion Type: Majority
Author: Judge Ruth Ancelet
Status: superseded
Overruled/Superseded By: Reyes v. Trustees of Ashworth University, 539 U.S. 306 (2003)
Topic: Narrow Tailoring - Plus-Factor Systems

========================================================================================

Procedural History
------------------

This declaratory judgment action was brought by Brennan City University itself, seeking
a determination that its admissions policy complies with the Equal Protection Clause as
construed in Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974)
(fictional), and distinguished in Delgado v. Trent Institute of Technology, 538 F.2d 902
(3d Cir. 1976) (fictional), after a local advocacy organization threatened suit. The
University sought guidance in light of uncertainty following Delgado about which forms
of race-conscious admissions remain lawful.

Facts
-----

Brennan City University's admissions office reviews every application through a single
process. Reviewers record race as one of approximately forty data points considered for
each applicant, alongside academic record, work experience, geographic origin,
socioeconomic background, and extracurricular achievement. No seats are reserved for any
group, no separate review track exists, and the University's admissions manual expressly
instructs reviewers not to assign race a fixed numerical weight.

Issue(s) Presented
------------------

Whether an admissions policy that considers race within a single, unified,
individualized review process -- without any separate track or fixed numerical target --
satisfies the narrow-tailoring requirement left open by Whitfield and applied in
Delgado.

Standard of Review
------------------

This case is governed by strict scrutiny, under which a race-conscious government policy
is sustained only if it is narrowly tailored to further a compelling governmental
interest.

Legal Analysis
--------------

The court reads Delgado not as a broad prohibition on race-conscious admissions but as a
narrower holding limited to fixed, separately administered set-asides. Because Brennan
City University's policy has none of the features the Delgado panel found objectionable
-- no separate track, no reserved seats, no fixed numerical target -- the court
concludes the policy falls on the permissible side of the line Whitfield and Delgado
together describe.

The court places particular weight on the University's admissions manual, which
instructs reviewers to consider race as one component of a holistic assessment and
expressly forbids assigning race a predetermined numerical value. The court finds this
documentary evidence of process meaningfully distinguishes Brennan City's program from
the Institute's program in Delgado, where the separate track and fixed seat count made
the set-aside's mechanical operation apparent from the record itself.

The court also credits testimony from the University's admissions director describing
how a representative file is evaluated, and finds the described process genuinely
individualized: reviewers read each application as a whole rather than sorting
applicants into predetermined racial categories before assessing other qualifications.

The court cautions that its holding rests on the specific features of Brennan City's
policy as documented in this record, and that a policy which drifts toward the fixed,
mechanical features condemned in Delgado -- whether through informal practice or a
change in the admissions manual -- would raise a different question.

Discussion of Prior Cases
-------------------------

* Delgado v. Trent Institute of Technology, 538 F.2d 902 (3d Cir. 1976) -- this opinion distinguishes that authority. Distinguishes the institute's fixed set-aside from brennan city's unified, individualized review process.
* Whitfield v. Board of Regents of Milford University, 417 U.S. 226 (1974) -- this opinion follows that authority. Follows whitfield's approval of race as one factor among many within an individualized process.

Holding
-------

The district court holds that Brennan City University's policy, because it treats race
as genuinely one factor among many within a single unified process, is distinguishable
from the fixed set-aside struck down in Delgado and satisfies narrow tailoring.

Conclusion
----------

The court declares that Brennan City University's admissions policy, as documented in
this record, satisfies the narrow-tailoring requirement of Whitfield and Delgado.
